# The Eight

Atlas's board: **eight cyborg executives**, the "wicked gods" of Blackbird's framing. Each is a boss with its own
unlock chain (milestone M5). Seven embody a **deadly sin** and run a **real Atlas business line**; the eighth is the
CEO.

**Everything below is PROPOSED except the structure** (seven sins + CEO, each a business line, CEO's backstory).
Names and gimmicks are drafts; the author vetoes. Names follow [`naming.md`](naming.md): grand, plausible, slightly
too much.

## Design rules for a board member

1. **Sin = corporate department.** The sin is the department's *business model*, not just a vibe. Greed runs the
   premiums. Wrath runs the dragons.
2. **They believe they're the good guys** (mostly). The comedy is sincerity. Menace comes from competence, not cruelty.
3. **Cyborg, with a gimmick the player can see.** Each has a visible chrome tell tied to the sin.
4. **Each has a signature "protection" product** that Blackbird will encounter in the world before she meets them.
5. **Each is beatable in a way that's thematically ironic** (the sin undoes them), and should be fought in a place
   that expresses their business line.
6. Keep each board member distinct in *voice*: see the "Sounds like" lines.

## The CEO: Power Corrupts

**Eighth board member, runs the whole show.** She is Blackbird's **mirror**:

- Once an idealistic, anti-Atlas crusader. A cyberknight, essentially, with the same fire as Blackbird.
- She got inside to fix it, made compromises, and the power rewrote her. She now sincerely believes Atlas is
  protecting people, and that her old self was naive.
- Never a cartoon villain: she's *tragic and a little unhinged by control*. She's what Blackbird becomes if she
  wins the wrong way.
- Her gimmick is an **old, battered cyberknight kit** among her gleaming chrome: a deliberate visual echo.
- **Working name (PROPOSED):** "Marguerite Aldous-Tern" (placeholder). Consider her old crusader callsign being
  something Blackbird would respect.
- She is the final boss and the emotional payoff. Blackbird's hatred of Atlas is the one constant, so the fight is
  about whether she can still hate someone who *used to be her*.
- Tone: grim-adjacent, but keep to the hard rules in [`README.md`](README.md): rueful and absurd, never bleak.
