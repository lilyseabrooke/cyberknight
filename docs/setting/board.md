# The Eight

Atlas's board: **eight cyborg executives**, the "wicked gods" of Blackbird's framing. Each is a boss with its own
unlock chain (milestone M5). Seven embody a **deadly sin** and run a **real Atlas business line**; the eighth is the
CEO.

**Canon:** the structure (seven sins + CEO, each a business line), the CEO's backstory, and the governance and
freeform rules below. **PROPOSED:** the names and gimmicks, which are drafts the author vetoes. Names follow [`naming.md`](naming.md): grand, plausible, slightly
too much.

## Design rules for a board member

1. **Sin = corporate department.** The sin is the department's *business model*, not just a vibe. Greed runs the
   premiums. Wrath runs the dragons.
2. **They believe they're the good guys** (mostly). The comedy is sincerity. Menace comes from competence, not cruelty.
3. **Cyborg, with a gimmick the player can see.** Each has a visible chrome tell tied to the sin.
4. **Each has a signature "protection" product** that Blackbird will encounter in the world before she meets them.
5. **There is no single way to beat a board member.** The game is freeform: Blackbird can fight, rob, expose,
   seduce, bankrupt, outmaneuver or humiliate them, however a given run goes. Write each member as a *person with
   weak points the sin creates* (Greed can be bought, Pride can be embarrassed), never as a scripted encounter.
   Content may offer hooks; it must not require one path. Locations are fluid too: don't pin a boss to a fixed place.
6. Keep each board member distinct in *voice*: see the "Sounds like" lines.

## How the board works

The seven **report to the CEO openly**, and she runs the company. But most decisions are made **with the board as
equals, like a real board**: votes, committees, quiet coalitions, rivalries over budget. Write boardroom scenes as
real corporate politics with the sins as personalities. Rank is real; so is the horse-trading.

**There is no order.** The board can be taken on in any order, in any way, depending on what Blackbird does. Don't
write content that assumes a member is fought, or even met, before or after another. Remaining members can react
to fallen ones.

## The CEO: Power Corrupts

**Eighth board member, runs the whole show.** She is Blackbird's **mirror**:

- Once an idealistic, anti-Atlas crusader. A cyberknight, essentially, with the same fire as Blackbird.
- She got inside to fix it, made compromises, and the power rewrote her. She now sincerely believes Atlas is
  protecting people, and that her old self was naive.
- Never a cartoon villain: she's *tragic and a little unhinged by control*. She's what Blackbird becomes if she
  wins the wrong way.
- Her gimmick is an **old, battered cyberknight kit** among her gleaming chrome: a deliberate visual echo.
- **Working name (PROPOSED):** "Marguerite Aldous-Tern" (placeholder). Consider her old crusader callsign being
  something Blackbird would respect.
- **Blackbird knows her past from the get-go** and takes it as a personal offense: a betrayal of what a cyberknight
  is. It is not a late reveal. Her hatred of the CEO is personal, not just corporate.
- She is the emotional payoff. Blackbird's hatred of Atlas is the one constant, so the question is whether she can
  still hate someone who *used to be her*. Like the rest of the board, she has no single required way to be beaten.
- Tone: grim-adjacent, but keep to the hard rules in [`README.md`](README.md): rueful and absurd, never bleak.
